@@ 33d3b054f1 html
The Federal Reserve (Fed) has two goals: stable prices and full employment. Each of its decisions can be read as weighing these two goals against each other. When inflation is well above target, it tolerates more weakness in jobs; when jobs are clearly deteriorating and inflation is near target, it turns to easing faster.

@@ e33f1f0684 html

The yen and the carry trade
@@ cad1fe10d5 html
Japan kept interest rates extremely low for a long time, so many investors borrowed yen, converted it into dollars or other currencies, and bought higher-yielding assets. That is the carry trade. As long as the yen holds steady or weakens, the trade earns the rate differential every day.
@@ e70e7eb256 html
The risk is a sudden, sharp rise in the yen. The debt that yen borrowers must repay grows instantly, so they have to sell whatever assets they hold to unwind, even assets that have nothing to do with one another. In late July 2024 the Bank of Japan raised rates; soon after, weak US jobs data in early August sent the yen soaring, and carry trades were unwound en masse. On August 5 the Nikkei fell about 12% in a single day, the VIX briefly spiked above 60 intraday, and popular US tech stocks were sold heavily. The market recovered most of the losses within a few weeks, but those few days of volatility were enough to knock out highly leveraged positions.
@@ 2212182bf1 html
Treat a sharp rise in the yen over a short period as an alarm for risk assets worldwide, even if you don't own any Japanese assets.

@@ c0f8104658 html
Crude has to go through a refinery to become gasoline, diesel and jet fuel. @@ 32d4d35937 html
Crude often meets clear resistance or speeds up near round numbers.
