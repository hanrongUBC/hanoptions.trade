The two legs of a stock price: earnings and the discount rate

The price of any stock can be split into two parts:
@@ 60a7ec2fc7 html
Almost every piece of macro news ends up landing on these two legs. Rising oil raises companies' costs and squeezes earnings; it also pushes up inflation, makes it harder for the central bank to cut rates, and lifts the discount rate. A stronger dollar shrinks multinationals' overseas revenue once it is converted back, and tightens funding conditions around the world. Weakening jobs first make the market hope for rate cuts, then make it worry about earnings.

Why stock and options traders need macro too
@@ ea4ec29e37 html
In 2022, many large tech companies whose earnings were still growing saw their shares fall anywhere from 30% to more than half. The companies hadn't gotten worse; their valuation multiples had. Rates rose quickly from near zero, and the price the market was willing to pay for the same dollar of earnings dropped sharply. That year, stock-picking skill could hardly offset the across-the-board selloff that macro brought.
@@ f8d412fdb0 html
When macro dominates the market, the correlation between individual stocks rises noticeably: everything goes up together and down together. Then however deep your research on a company, it only decides how much less you lose than everyone else. For options traders it is even more direct: rates, the dollar and data days change implied volatility (IV) across the whole market, and with it the price of every option.
