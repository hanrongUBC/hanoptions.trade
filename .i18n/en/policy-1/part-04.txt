A price elasticity of demand of 1.0 means sales fall 1% for every 1% rise in price. With the default settings, a 25% tariff raises costs by 7.5; the company passes through half by raising prices 3.75%, sales fall 3.75%, and gross profit drops about 12.8%. Drag elasticity to 0 and full pass-through preserves gross profit completely; drag it to 2.0 and gross profit falls 15% even with full pass-through. Companies with strong brands and hard-to-replace products have low elasticity. That is “pricing power.”

The last point is especially important. Tariff policy often follows a rhythm of “announce hard, then partly back down.” In April 2025, after sweeping tariffs were announced, stocks and Treasuries both fell sharply, and within a week most of the tariffs were paused for 90 days. When you trade tariff news, treat “a pause or an exemption” as a scenario that can appear at any moment: don't slash your positions in the first day's panic, and don't chase in the first day's cheering.
