Trade the policy, not the slogan
@@ 7ab59f1a74 html
Political news is the noisiest kind of information in the market. But beneath the noise are things that really change where money flows: budgets, laws, tariffs, export restrictions, subsidies and procurement contracts. This handbook cares only about the latter.

The difference between a slogan and a policy
@@ 02e005415b html
A politician says, “We're going to bring the factories back.” That's a slogan. Congress passes a law that subsidizes companies building plants at home, the Treasury issues the detailed rules, companies announce investment plans and engineering firms win contracts. That's policy, and the capital flows it creates. The first may be forgotten the next day; the second shows up in earnings reports for years.

Why set your political views aside
@@ 967eeae6f5 html
Everyone has political views, and that's normal. The problem is that political views quietly creep into your positions. When the side you support takes office, you tend to believe its policies will all succeed; when the side you oppose takes office, you tend to think the economy is headed for trouble. Both tendencies lead you astray.
@@ b84d222c71 html
The market doesn't care whom you support. It cares only which companies' revenue a policy will raise and which companies' costs it will push up. A useful test: if the side you support loses power, would your positions keep you up at night? If so, you're betting on a belief, not on logic you can test.
