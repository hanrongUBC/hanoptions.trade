<span class="sn">14.1</span>The nine-step process
@@ bf6f6a37e5 html
The process makes every trade answer the same nine questions. Only when the questions are the same can you compare results across trades and trace a mistake to a specific step.
@@ 75c6bc306b html
Options trading is an environment of incomplete answers: a correct decision can lose money, and a wrong one can make money. From the P&amp;L of a single trade you can't tell good judgment from good luck. The process breaks a trade into nine stages, each with a clear output, so in review you can ask “which step went wrong?” instead of just saying “this one lost.”

Beginners spend most of their time on steps 1 and 8: finding opportunities, watching the screen, deciding when to sell. Long-run results depend more on steps 3, 5 and 7. The structure decides in which scenarios you make money, the size decides whether you survive the worst scenario, and the management rules decide whether losses get out of hand. All three are done before the order goes in and take almost no screen time.
