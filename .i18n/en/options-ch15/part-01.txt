Three amplified tendencies
@@ cf36df3809 html
<strong>Overweighting small probabilities.</strong> People tend to feel “small chance, big payoff” events as weightier than their true probability. An option at $0.15 that might become $1.50 is too vivid a picture: a 10% chance feels like 30%. That makes buyers willing to overpay for OTM and 0DTE options.
@@ 6b3680babc html
<strong>Drawing certainty from small samples.</strong> A seller with a 90% win rate often wins a dozen or more times in a row. People judge risk by recent experience, and after a dozen-plus trades without trouble they feel “nothing will go wrong.” But that 10% of losses rarely shows up in a sample of a dozen or so to begin with (Chapter 14: the probability of not seeing a 5% tail in 28 trades is 23.8%).
@@ 28cd42f256 html
<strong>Loss aversion and the disposition effect.</strong> People tend to take profits early and hang on to losers. Hanging on to a stock at worst means a slow decline; hanging on to an option also means facing expiration and Gamma: the longer a seller holds on, the bigger the Delta gets, and the longer a buyer holds on, the less time value is left.
