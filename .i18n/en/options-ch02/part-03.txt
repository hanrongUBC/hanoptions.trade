Order types
@@ bd0a7aa0fc html
<strong>Market orders</strong>: avoid them for options. When spreads are wide, or quotes are unstable in the first few minutes after the open, a market order can fill at an absurd price.
@@ 1901915f56 html
<strong>Limit orders</strong>: the standard approach is to start at the mid and, if you don't get filled, move a cent or a few cents toward the other side every 30 seconds to a minute. This is called “walking the order.”
@@ 5b95e32820 html
<strong>Combo orders</strong>: multi-leg trades such as spreads and straddles are submitted as a single order at a net price, and the exchange fills all the legs at once. Placing the legs separately (“legging in”) risks one leg filling while the price of the other runs away.
@@ e149886737 html
<strong>Stop orders</strong>: stop orders on options are usually triggered by the option's own price, so a momentary widening of the spread can trigger one by mistake and fill it at a terrible price. Many traders use alerts on the underlying stock's price instead and then place limit orders by hand.

<span class="sn">2.8</span>Tax concepts
@@ 8803c7d3be html
How options are taxed depends on the underlying: broad-based index options get the Section 1256 60/40 treatment, while stock and ETF options are taxed as ordinary capital gains. The wash sale rule applies to options too.
@@ 2d7da1ac1c html
This section covers only general concepts that apply to US tax residents, to help you understand why traders care about these distinctions. The actual treatment depends on your status, income, state and that year's rules; if you are not a US tax resident, the rules may be entirely different. For an actual return, consult a tax professional.
@@ a0eb649331 html
Section 1256 contracts and 60/40
@@ 36e19298ad html
Broad-based index options such as SPX, XSP, NDX and RUT (as well as futures) are “Section 1256 contracts,” with two special rules:
@@ 27c6cbbdb9 html
<strong>60/40</strong>: however long you hold, gains and losses are taxed as 60% long-term and 40% short-term capital gains.
@@ 064f80b763 html
<strong>Year-end mark-to-market</strong>: positions still open on the last trading day of December are treated as sold at that day's market value, so unrealized gains and losses also count for that year.
@@ 7a196376ba html
Options on ETFs such as SPY and QQQ generally aren't Section 1256 contracts; gains and losses on positions held a year or less are taxed as short-term capital gains. For people who trade often and hold briefly, the difference can be large.
@@ 9abef5f8d7 html
Example (tax rates are assumptions): short-term capital gains are taxed at 32%, long-term at 15%. The blended 60/40 rate is <span class="num">0.6 × 15% + 0.4 × 32% = 21.8%</span>. On the same $10,000 of short-term profit, SPX options would owe about $2,180 and SPY options about $3,200, a difference of $1,020. In the top federal bracket, the two rates are <span class="num">26.8%</span> and 37% (not counting state taxes and other surtaxes).

The wash sale rule
@@ a76abe201f html
If you sell a security at a loss and buy a “substantially identical” security within 30 days before or after the sale, the loss can't be deducted now; instead it is added to the cost basis of the new position and deferred. For options, this matters in two ways:
@@ ec2700fb55 html
Buying a call on the same stock within 30 days after selling the stock at a loss counts as acquiring an option to buy that stock and generally triggers a wash sale.
@@ 9931009b4d html
Options can also trigger it among themselves: you close a call at a loss, then within 30 days buy a call on the same underlying with similar terms. In some cases it isn't clearly defined which options count as “substantially identical.”
@@ be1d24b9b0 html
Example: on October 1 you sell 100 shares of XYZ at a $1,500 loss, and on October 15 you buy 1 XYZ call. That $1,500 loss generally can't be deducted now; it is added to the cost of the call instead. Also, for shares you receive through assignment, the premium goes into the cost basis: if you sold a 90 put for 0.40 and were assigned, the tax basis of the shares is 89.60.
