<span class="sn">12.4</span>The three directions of rolling
@@ 0af2ff953a html
A roll is two trades: buy back the old position and sell a new one. The three directions differ in what you give up in exchange for time and room.
@@ 745bc878ce html
<strong>Rolling out</strong>: keep the strike and move to a later expiration. You sell more time and can usually collect a net credit; the price is that the risk lasts longer.
@@ 213afa6f00 html
<strong>Rolling down / up</strong>: keep the expiration and change the strike. Roll short puts down and short calls up, moving the strike away from the current price. Within the same expiration, a strike further away is cheaper, so this always costs money.
@@ ec5cdf0812 html
<strong>Rolling out and down</strong>: both at once. The extra time value pays for moving the strike down, often at close to zero cost.
