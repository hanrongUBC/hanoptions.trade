What you assume when you add up Theta and Vega
@@ edaed6a4b5 html
Theta added up is “how much more you'll collect by tomorrow if everything else stays the same.” That “everything else stays the same” is the key: it's only a reference value, and the market changes every day.
@@ 159f4fe5f4 html
Adding Vega directly assumes every underlying's IV moves by the same number of points at the same time. In reality that rarely holds: when the market falls, single-stock IVs tend to rise by more points than SPY's IV, because they start out higher. In the opening portfolio, the two SPY legs' Vega totals <span class="num">−$111.0</span> and the A and D positions total <span class="num">−$75.2</span>, for a simple sum of <span class="num">−$186.3</span>. If you assume single-stock IVs move 1.5 times as many points as SPY's, then per 1-point move in SPY, the portfolio's Vega is really <span class="num">−111.0 + 1.5 × (−75.2) = −$223.9</span>, 20% bigger than the headline number.
