Source 5: the volatility risk premium
@@ 197848305b html
A large body of research finds that the implied volatility of index options is, on average, higher than the volatility realized afterward. The reason isn't hard to see: investors are willing to overpay for insurance, and sellers demand compensation for bearing crash risk. This persistent gap is called the <strong>volatility risk premium</strong>, and it is the main source of sellers' long-run returns.
@@ 7932430917 html

For a stock that has just surged, a common approach is to sell out-of-the-money calls rather than buy puts. The reasoning maps directly onto this section's categories. A long put earns direction money: it needs the stock to fall, and soon, and even two weeks of sideways trading near the highs gets eaten by time decay.
