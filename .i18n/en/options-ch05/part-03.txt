Vanna: two readings of one number
@@ 6ed6978890 html
Chapter 4 showed that the Delta of a 30-day 110 call rises with IV, by about 0.009 for each 1-point rise. Look at it the other way: when the stock goes from 100 to 101, this option’s Vega goes from <span class="num">0.0675</span> to <span class="num">0.0763</span>, an increase of 0.0087. Computed by central differences, both numbers are <span class="num">0.00873</span>, exactly equal. This is no coincidence: both are the cross second derivative of price with respect to <span class="v">S</span> and σ, and the order of differentiation can be swapped.
@@ 325f1e8d8d html
In practice: as the stock moves toward an OTM option’s strike, the option’s IV exposure grows. An ATM option’s Vanna is close to 0; the ITM 90 call has negative Vanna (−0.0069), so its Vega actually shrinks as the stock rises.

Charm: theta and delta tell the same story
@@ 39c837f657 html
After one day, the 110 call (30 days) sees its Delta fall from 0.152 by <span class="num">0.0047</span>. At the same time, for every $1 the stock rises, its daily Theta takes an extra <span class="num">0.0047</span>. Again, this is the same cross derivative. The ITM 90 call is the mirror image: its Delta drifts toward 1 by 0.0033 a day. Chapter 4 covered the expiration-day hedging flows that Charm creates; what we add here is its link to Theta: the more sensitive a position’s Theta is to the stock price, the faster its Delta drifts over time.

Volga: OTM options gain “extra” when IV spikes
@@ 4e94f614c7 html
Vega extrapolates linearly from a 1-point change in IV. An ATM option’s price is almost a straight line in IV, so the extrapolation is accurate; an OTM option’s price curves upward in IV, so when IV jumps, the actual gain far exceeds what Vega predicts.

When IV doubles from 30% to 60%, the 85 put’s actual price is 2.5 times the Vega estimate. This is one reason far-OTM puts serve as crash insurance: in a crisis they benefit from Delta, Gamma, Vega and Volga all at once. It is also why sellers of far-OTM options lose far more than expected in a crisis: negative Volga means that the more IV rises, the bigger your Vega exposure gets. To put a number on it: sell 10 contracts of the 30-day 85 put and collect $81; with the stock unchanged and IV doubling from 30% to 60%, the loss is <span class="num">$1,328</span>, while the Vega estimate is only <span class="num">0.0164 × 30 × 1,000 = $492</span>. Stress-testing a short position with Vega understates tail risk by more than half. You have to reprice it in full.
