Distortion in very short-dated options
@@ e261ded331 html
The shorter the term, the bigger the gap between the two clocks. Suppose a stock’s true volatility, measured in trading days, is 30%. On Friday you buy an option expiring Monday: 1 day left on the trading-day clock, 3 days on the calendar-day clock. An IV point is not the same across terms
@@ cfe4403416 html
Vega answers “what is a 1-point move in IV worth,” but IV at different expirations doesn’t move by the same amount. When the market gets a scare, 30-day IV might jump 10 points while one-year IV moves only three or four: short-term IV is like the waves, long-term IV like the tide. Comparing the one-year Vega of 0.383 directly with the 30-day 0.114 overstates the IV risk of long-dated options.
@@ 5b5306eb6e html
A common fix is “time-weighted Vega”: Vega × √(30 / days), which assumes that IV at a <span class="v">T</span>-day term moves √(30/<span class="v">T</span>) times as much as 30-day IV. On this basis, ATM options at 7, 30, 90 and 365 days come out at <span class="num">0.114</span>, <span class="num">0.114</span>, <span class="num">0.113</span> and <span class="num">0.110</span>, almost the same. The square-root relationship is only a rule of thumb, and how closely short- and long-term IV move together varies a lot from period to period. @@ 96f3badd87 html

@@ 2ea3604641 html

@@ d47bf020db html
