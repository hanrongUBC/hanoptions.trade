First, learn to count in R
@@ 1f77aab6cb html
R is the risk you plan to take on a trade. If you buy at $50 with a stop-loss at $47, you're risking $3 per share; that is 1R. A $6 gain counts as +2R, and getting stopped out counts as −1R. Counting in R puts trades at different prices and sizes on the same ruler.
@@ 26c704fd26 html
A system's long-run value is set by its expected value (EV):
@@ 15b731b8b3 html
<span class="num">EV (R) = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss</span>
@@ 8d07aa6dc1 html
For example, with a 45% win rate, an average win of 2R and an average loss of 1R: <span class="num">0.45 × 2 − 0.55 × 1 = +0.35R</span>. Over time, each trade earns on average an extra 0.35 times the amount at risk. That is quite a good system.

Being right is not the same as making money
@@ 504910fa90 html
The same correct call can get you shaken out midway because the position is too big; it can end in “right direction, but the option died first” because the option was too short-dated; or it can catch only the tail end because you got in too late. Your final P&amp;L is the product of four things: judgment, timing, instrument and size. If any one of them is zero, the result is zero.
