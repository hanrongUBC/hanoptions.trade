<span class="sn">13.1</span>Three ways to measure a position
@@ d95e5a3d5a html
Measure an options position with three rulers: notional exposure, max loss and Greek risk. Premium is a fit measure only for buyers.
@@ 9b3e49b48a html
<strong>Notional exposure</strong>: shares covered by the contracts × stock price. It tells you how much stock you could have to take on or owe in the worst case, and it is the base for assignment and margin.
@@ e83ee9a841 html
<strong>Max loss</strong>: the worst outcome at expiry. Spreads and iron condors have a hard cap; a naked short put is capped at “strike − premium” (the stock goes to zero); a naked short call has no cap.
@@ d44cc9e5af html
<strong>Greek risk</strong>: Delta dollars (Δ × 100 × stock price) tell you how much stock the position is equivalent to; Vega tells you how much you make or lose for each point of IV. Put them through a realistic shock (a price gap plus an IV spike) and you get the stress loss.

@@ d3f6f72b62 html
<strong>Buyers</strong>: the premium is the max loss, so measuring by premium is right. Under the same shock the long call loses only $289, less than the premium: the doubling of IV wins part of it back.
@@ a53e3742c4 html
<strong>Sellers</strong>: the premium is the smallest number on the three rulers. The short 90 put collects $100; its stress loss is 12.6 times that, and its max loss 89 times.
@@ ccdd7fc5a0 html

@@ 7f184485ae html
