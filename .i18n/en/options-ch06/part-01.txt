<span class="sn">6.1</span>How to calculate realized volatility
@@ 38a0715ae6 html
Realized volatility (also called actual or historical volatility, written RV) is the standard deviation of log returns, scaled up to a year by the square root of time.
@@ b9d87d8108 html
σ<sub>daily</sub> is the sample standard deviation of daily log returns over a period (dividing by n − 1). 252 is the number of trading days in a year.

@@ e95b886485 html
Six consecutive closing prices: 100, 101.5, 100.8, 102.2, 101.0, 101.9. The five log returns are <span class="num">+1.489%</span>, <span class="num">−0.692%</span>, <span class="num">+1.379%</span>, <span class="num">−1.181%</span> and <span class="num">+0.887%</span>, averaging <span class="num">+0.376%</span>. Many platforms and traders assume a mean of zero and use √(Σ<span class="v">r</span>²/<span class="v">n</span>) directly. For this data that gives a daily volatility of <span class="num">1.164%</span>, or <span class="num">18.49%</span> annualized. Over a short window the mean is mostly noise, so assuming zero is actually more stable; the two methods diverge noticeably only when there is a strong trend. When comparing RV from different sources, first check which method each one uses.

Why multiply by the square root
@@ cc100871af html
If daily returns are independent, the variance of the cumulative return over <span class="v">n</span> days is <span class="v">n</span> times the one-day variance, and the standard deviation is √<span class="v">n</span> times. So to annualize you multiply by √252, for a week by √5, and for a month (about 21 trading days) by √21. The assumption breaks down in two cases: when prices show a clear trend or mean reversion (returns are correlated from one day to the next), and when there are gaps (one day’s variance far exceeds the others’). When it breaks down, square-root scaling understates or overstates volatility over longer periods.

@@ 834b6fe14f html
IV in Black-Scholes counts time in calendar days (<span class="v">T</span> = days / 365), while RV is annualized over trading days (× √252). Both are “annualized” numbers and can be compared directly. You only need to tell them apart when converting to “per day”: per trading day, use ÷√252; per calendar day, ÷√365. There is almost no price movement over weekends, so daily volatility measured in trading days is closer to what you see on your screen. This is also where Chapter 4’s “divide by 16” and “divide by 19” come from.
