<span class="sn">6.3</span>The volatility risk premium
@@ dfa19bae5f html
Over the long run, the implied volatility of index options is higher than the realized volatility that follows. This gap is called the volatility risk premium (VRP). It is the seller’s source of income and the seller’s reward for bearing tail risk.
@@ 017b36acc9 html
Take the S&amp;P 500: on long-run average, VIX runs a few percentage points above the realized volatility of the following 30 days. That is only an average: in the month a crisis breaks out, realized volatility can far exceed the implied volatility beforehand.
@@ c561198563 html
Where the premium comes from
@@ f5ee70ed91 html
<strong>Demand for insurance</strong>: pension funds, mutual funds and other institutions hold large amounts of stock and keep buying index puts to protect their portfolios. This demand isn’t very sensitive to price.
@@ 460b0e1b58 html
<strong>Crash aversion</strong>: people will pay more than the expected loss to avoid a big loss, just as they do with home insurance.
@@ 129a2abdb8 html
<strong>Sellers need capital</strong>: whoever sells options ties up margin and bears occasional huge losses. Without enough compensation, nobody would take that side.

<span class="sn">6.4</span>IV Rank and IV Percentile
@@ ac11701b23 html
Whether IV is high or low in absolute terms means little; what matters is where it sits in its own history. IV Rank measures where the current value sits between the one-year high and low; IV Percentile measures how many days in the past year were lower than now.

@@ 5f67e615d2 html
